1 Executive Summary & Thesis Motivation

Autonomous Vehicles (AVs) navigating dense, chaotic urban environments face profound safety challenges when interacting with Vulnerable Road Users (VRUs), including pedestrians, bicyclists, e-scooter riders, and skateboarders. Despite substantial advancements in 2D/3D object detection, behavioral intention prediction remains the most critical vulnerability leading to fatal edge-case collisions.

The Fundamental Limitation of Existing SOTA: Leading pedestrian intention models (such as IntentFormer, PedX, and PV-LSTM) frame behavior recognition as a primitive, binary classification task: "Will the pedestrian cross? (Yes/No)". In real-world urban traffic, binary prediction is dangerously insufficient. An autonomous vehicle cannot plan collision-free trajectories without recognizing fine-grained transitions (e.g., decelerating at the curb vs. accelerating into the street), micromobility dynamics, and relational multi-agent interactions (e.g., a pedestrian yielding to an approaching bicycle). Furthermore, existing deep networks operate as uninterpretable black boxes, precluding post-incident diagnostic validation and safety-critical certification.

Thesis Thesis Proposition: This thesis conceives, implements, and empirically validates X-MIST (Explainable Multimodal Interaction-aware Spatio-Temporal Transformer), a foundation architecture that simultaneously resolves the tri-fold challenge of:

  • Fine-Grained Behavioral Recognition: Simultaneous multi-task classification across 8 pedestrian micro-actions, 9 micromobility dynamics, and 6 relational multi-agent interactions.
  • Dynamic Interaction-Aware Modeling ($G_t$): Graph neural spatial message passing capturing distance, relative velocity, and Time-to-Collision (TTC) between heterogeneous traffic participants.
  • Space-Time-Modality-Interaction Explainability (XAI): Inherently faithful mathematical attribution identifying critical temporal inflection points ($I_t$), sensor contribution ratios ($M_m$), and relational graph attention ($A_{ij}$), accompanied by human-readable natural language diagnostic reports.

3 Complete System Architecture & End-to-End Pipeline

The X-MIST architecture processes continuous urban video streams discretized into temporal windows of length $T=32$ frames ($\Delta t = 3.2\, ext{s}$ at $10\, ext{FPS}$). The complete computational flow encompasses four sequential stages: (i) Multi-Stream Modality Encoding, (ii) Dynamic Modality Gating & Cross-Modal Fusion, (iii) Dynamic Relational Interaction Graph Convolution, and (iv) Spatio-Temporal Transformer Classification with Integrated XAI Attribution.

4 Mathematical Formulation & Sub-Module Mechanics

4.1 Modality Encoding & Dynamic Gating

Each sensory stream is independently mapped into a synchronized embedding space $\mathbf{z}^m_t \in \mathbb{R}^d$ ($d=256$) for all temporal indices $t \in [1, \dots, T]$:

\mathbf{z}^{ ext{rgb}}_t = f_{ ext{rgb}}(\mathbf{X}^{ ext{rgb}}_t), \quad \mathbf{z}^{ ext{pose}}_t = f_{ ext{pose}}(\mathbf{X}^{ ext{pose}}_t), \quad \mathbf{z}^{ ext{traj}}_t = f_{ ext{traj}}(\mathbf{X}^{ ext{traj}}_t), \quad \mathbf{z}^{ ext{scene}}_t = f_{ ext{scene}}(\mathbf{X}^{ ext{scene}}_t)

To prevent performance degradation during partial sensor dropouts (e.g., camera lens glare, skeletal joint occlusion), we introduce a Dynamic Modality Gating Unit (MGU):

\mathbf{g}_t = \sigma\left(\mathbf{W}_g \cdot [\mathbf{z}^{ ext{rgb}}_t \,\|\, \mathbf{z}^{ ext{pose}}_t \,\|\, \mathbf{z}^{ ext{traj}}_t \,\|\, \mathbf{z}^{ ext{scene}}_t] + \mathbf{b}_g ight) \in [0, 1]^4, \quad \mathbf{ ilde{z}}^m_t = g_{m, t} \cdot \mathbf{z}^m_t

Where $\sigma(\cdot)$ is the Sigmoid activation and $\mathbf{W}_g \in \mathbb{R}^{4 imes 4d}$ parameterizes dynamic modality confidence weighting.

4.2 Dynamic Relational Interaction Graph ($G_t$)

Unlike conventional models that isolate the primary pedestrian, X-MIST represents the traffic scene as a dynamic relational graph $G_t = (V_t, E_t)$ where node $v_0 \in V_t$ represents the target agent and nodes $\{v_1, \dots, v_K\}$ represent nearby traffic participants. 5 Multi-Task Behavioral Taxonomy & Classification Targets

The core contribution of this thesis is transitioning autonomous driving from binary classification to fine-grained action and relational reasoning. The model optimizes three simultaneous behavioral heads:

Taxonomy Head Num Classes Class Names & Physical Safety Definition
Task A: Pedestrian Behaviors 8 Classes Walking (longitudinal movement along sidewalk), Standing (stationary), Stopping (deceleration to halt), Starting (acceleration from stop into crosswalk), Turning (angular heading shift), Crossing (traversing roadway), Yielding (pausing for vehicular traffic), Avoiding (evasive collision avoidance maneuver).
Task B: Micromobility Behaviors 9 Classes Moving, Stopping, Starting, Turning, Accelerating, Decelerating, Yielding, Avoiding, Overtaking. (Governs bicycles, e-scooters, and micro-transit sharing the road).
Task C: Relational Interactions 6 Classes Approaching (closing spatial separation), Yielding (conceding right-of-way), Avoiding (lateral swerve), Overtaking (passing), Conflict ($ ext{TTC} < 2.0\, ext{s}$ with overlapping trajectories), Cooperative movement (synchronized crossing).

6 Multi-Task Loss Formulation & XAI Regularization

Autonomous driving datasets exhibit extreme class imbalance (e.g., thousands of walking samples vs. scarce evasive avoidance samples). To ensure stable convergence, X-MIST optimizes a multi-objective loss combining Multi-Class Focal Loss, Contrastive Cross-Modal Alignment, and Attention Sparsity Regularization:

\mathcal{L}_{ ext{total}} = \lambda_{ ext{ped}} \mathcal{L}_{ ext{FL}}(\hat{\mathbf{y}}_{ ext{ped}}, \mathbf{y}_{ ext{ped}}) + \lambda_{ ext{micro}} \mathcal{L}_{ ext{FL}}(\hat{\mathbf{y}}_{ ext{micro}}, \mathbf{y}_{ ext{micro}}) + \lambda_{ ext{inter}} \mathcal{L}_{ ext{FL}}(\hat{\mathbf{y}}_{ ext{inter}}, \mathbf{y}_{ ext{inter}}) + \lambda_{ ext{align}} \mathcal{L}_{ ext{align}} + \lambda_{ ext{xai}} \mathcal{L}_{ ext{entropy}}

Where the components are formulated as follows:

  • Multi-Class Focal Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{ ext{FL}}$): Dynamically down-weights well-classified common behaviors and focuses gradients on hard transition samples: $$\mathcal{L}_{ ext{FL}} = -\sum_{c=1}^{C} lpha_c (1 - p_c)^\gamma \log(p_c), \quad \gamma = 2.0$$
  • Contrastive Cross-Modal Alignment ($\mathcal{L}_{ ext{align}}$): Maximizes cosine similarity between positive synchronized multimodal embeddings while penalizing temporally mismatched frames (InfoNCE framework).
  • XAI Attention Sparsity Regularizer ($\mathcal{L}_{ ext{entropy}}$): Enforces temporal attention concentration so that model attribution cleanly isolates the crucial 0.3s–0.5s decision inflection point rather than diffusing uniformly across all 32 frames.

8 Empirical Validation on MANIT HPC (Real Autonomous Datasets)
